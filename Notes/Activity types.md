
***The notion of an activity type:***

any recognized activity, and I take the notion of something where members are well defined and have constrained on participants, setting. Like job interviews, a jural interrogation, or a football game.

***The cognitive dimension:***

mental expectation about a scenario, basically what happens there normally, who does it what types of language you would see and more. Generally the cognitive dimension is mostly about how situations are predictable and have proper linguistic patterns. 

***Interactable dimension:***

how people communicate with the scenario, based on social norms and signals made by other parties. For example, an interview where the interviewer will ask the questions, the interviewee is trying to prove himself, and politeness is expected more from the interviewee. This demonstrates the basic systems in place that make specific scenarios run the same, and that's the human idea of mimicking, we follow the patterns we think are standard so that scenarios proceed smoothly, and that creates a system.

General summary of what matters:

>Setting. Usually a formal environment, often connected to the interview (e.g. a job
interview would be in the workplace of the applied-for job, a police interview would
be at the police station). The interviewer is often positioned behind a desk.
 Participants and their social roles. One or more interviewer and usually one
interviewee. Adults, formally dressed.
 Goals. Interviewee: to secure the job. Interviewer: to appoint the best candidate for
the job.
 Social parameters: Asymmetric power relations (interviewer possesses institutional
power which overrides any other type of power possessed by the interviewee).
Usually strangers.
 Norms of information exchange (cf. Grice 1975). Interviewee/interviewer:
information exchanged is relevant to the job and the candidate‟s suitability for it;
Interviewee: the maxims of quantity and quality are relaxed.
 Politeness norms. Interviewee: modesty is relaxed (cf. Leech‟s Politeness Principle
1983).
 Possible discourse types. Interviewer: questioning/directing/summarising;
interviewee: explaining/justifying/exemplifying/elaborating.
 Conversation structure. Interviewee: takes longest turns and most turns. Interviewer:
initiates the interaction and controls the topic.
 Register. Formal speech, standard dialect.
 Norms of interpretation. Interviewee: takes questions and assertions as requests or
opportunities for elaboration. Interviewer: takes the management of answers as the
focal point of assessment of the candidate‟s suitability
